▶ Production bible

DrDad on Video

Series plan
& teleprompter

Document it once, point at the link.

Two shows, not one. A short evergreen course that explains DrDad one concept at a time, and a loose weekly devlog that shows the sausage being made. Both lean on the same move: don't tell people the tool is honest — show a gate catching a model in the act.

The posture is show-and-tell, not a pitch: "I built this to understand how agent loops work and it turned out useful." Cloud and Hybrid are the primary modes; Local is a resilience mode for a disconnected afternoon. Never frame local-only as the goal.

Before you hit record

Five production principles

1
Lead with the payoff, not the setup.

Open on the money shot — a gate catching a fabricated "done" — then explain. Never spend the first five minutes on install.

2
One concept per basics episode.

Short and modular (6–12 min) so each is linkable. The whole point is to say "watch the gates video," not re-explain it.

3
Show the failures.

A real spiral, a gate firing, a frontier model graded an A on broken code — that footage is the differentiator. Capture real runs; keep the good disasters.

4
Keep the weekly skeleton fixed.

Same five beats every week. A repeatable template is what keeps a devlog alive past month one; improvise the content, never the structure.

5
Repeat the anchor lines.

Say the same few framings in most episodes. Repetition across videos is what makes a concept stick; a line you say once is a line nobody remembers.

Track B

DrDad Devlog

weekly · loose

Same five beats every week — that fixed shape is what keeps it alive. Improvise the content, never the structure. 10–20 minutes; a webcam, a screen, and a real session.

00:00
Cold open — the one thing

The single most interesting thing that happened this week, in 30–60 s, before any hello. A catch, a ship, a failure.

01:00
What shipped & why it was earned

The version bump(s), and for each one the FAILURE that earned it. Open the CHANGELOG on screen — it reads as a field log.

04:00
In the chair

A real work session, screen shared. Pick one where a gate fires or something goes sideways — that's the content. Don't edit out the mess; narrate it.

14:00
Next & open questions

What's next and what you haven't solved — roadmap in the open. Invite run reports; the honest ask is the community hook.

16:00
Sign-off

The recurring anchor line. Same close every week so people know the show landed.

Your first devlog writes itself. Open the CHANGELOG for the real list: for example 0.57.0 (upgrade-project stopped rewriting a project's line endings, and an appended .gitignore entry no longer got glued onto an unterminated last line, which had left .env un-ignored), 0.58.0 (the mission restated: cloud and hybrid first, local as the resilience mode), and the cms3 run where a full /audit caught three bugs a frontier model had graded an A (Delete-405, an upload wired to an API that doesn't exist, a modal that crashes before it renders). Cold open on the Delete-405 curl, and you're off.

Teleprompter

Two cold opens, written out

The only fully-scripted parts. Read these; keep everything after loose. Red is a camera or action cue, not spoken.

Cold open — Trailer / E1

[straight to camera, no intro]

A top-tier AI model just built me a feature. Wrote the tests. Ran them. Graded its own work an A.

[cut to screen: the Delete button / the 405 in the terminal]

The feature was a delete button that returns a 405 and never deletes anything. The tests were green. The model was sure.

This is DrDad. It's a harness for agentic coding, and it runs on one stubborn rule: never accept an assertion a script can settle.

Models — even the best ones — will tell you the work is done. DrDad makes them prove it. And when they can't, it says so. [beat] Let me show you how.

Cold open — a Devlog week

[straight to camera]

This week DrDad caught three bugs a frontier model swore were done — on a real app, 68 of 70 tasks in, all graded an A.

[cut to screen: the grade card, A crossed out to C]

An upload wired to an editor API that doesn't exist. A dialog that crashes before it renders. And a delete button that never deletes. Green tests on all three.

That's the whole idea: prompts telling a model to behave don't hold; scripts that check do. Let's get into what shipped, and then I'll run a session live and we'll see what breaks.

Practical

Cadence & notes

▮
Order of filming.

Trailer + E1 first (they set the frame), then the gates episode (E5) — it's the strongest, and if only one basics video ever gets watched, that's the one.

▮
Source material is already on disk.

Your run transcripts (run15Sept26 and the earlier spirals), the grade cards, the CHANGELOG. The disasters are documented — you're editing, not staging.

▮
Keep it cheap to make.

Screen + webcam + the anchor lines. No intro animation budget. The candor is the production value; polish would fight the brand.

▮
Check the numbers before you say them.

Figures such as 68 of 70 tasks and 111 closes come from the cms3 run log, not from this repository. Confirm them against the transcript and grade cards before filming; the kit's own counts (267 checks, 17 commands, 18 agents) are computed in the repo at v0.58.0.

▮
Point everything at the two links.

Repo in every description; the Field Manual as the "read the overview" link. Both pages now live in the repo's overview/ folder, and GitHub is the source of truth. The videos exist so you stop re-explaining — so always hand people the next step.

▮
Miss a week without guilt.

The devlog is "when there's something worth showing," not a contract. A shipped gate or a good failure is the bar; a quiet week just waits.

DrDad on Video — production bible, companion to the DrDad Field Manual. Keep the anchor lines verbatim; keep everything else loose. The show is the same promise as the tool: show the work, prove the claim.
